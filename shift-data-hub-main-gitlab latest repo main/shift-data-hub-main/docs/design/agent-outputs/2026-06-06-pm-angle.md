**Author:** Alex (Product Manager agent)

## 1. Strategic Intent

This is not a "dashboard cleanup" project. It is **de-risking SHIFT's first paid-acquisition quarter by ensuring the people spending money can see what's working in time to act on it.** Paid X ads, sponsored content, and KOL deals start shipping this week — each of those is a real-money bet whose ROI is invisible without a hub that marketing and management can trust independently of Tomer. Today the hub serves one user who can debug it; tomorrow it will be the single source of truth in a paid-media standup. The row-multiplication incident proved the trust ceiling is exactly one person high. If we do nothing, three predictable failures land within 30 days: (a) marketing makes a budget-allocation call on wrong numbers and we either over-invest in a dead channel or kill a live one, (b) management starts maintaining a parallel spreadsheet "to be safe" and the hub becomes irrelevant within a quarter, (c) every data question routes through Tomer, who becomes the bottleneck on a 5-person org's daily decisions. The business outcome we're buying is **decision velocity without decision risk** during the most expensive acquisition window the company has ever run.

## 2. Phased Plan (Prioritization Rationale)

Sized by what unblocks the next phase — not by calendar.

### Phase 1 — Earn the right to be opened (Trust Floor)
**Outcome:** Marketing and management can open the hub Monday morning without Tomer pre-flighting the numbers.
**Success criteria:**
- Zero P0 data-correctness incidents during a 2-week internal soak period.
- Every Level-1 number on the default tab has a documented derivation + a reconciliation test that fails CI if the number drifts >threshold.
- "Last validated" timestamp visible on the highest-traffic tab so users see when trust was last confirmed.
**Dependencies:** None. Independent of audience research, independent of UI changes. Runs in parallel with Phase 2 discovery.
**Effort:** M (engineering-heavy, but scoped — the top ~15 numbers, not all ~150).
**Risk if skipped:** The next row-multiplication ships to a marketer in a meeting. Trust is lost in one incident and takes a quarter to rebuild — if ever. This phase is non-negotiable and must be the first thing that ships, regardless of what the audience research surfaces.

### Phase 2 — Make it answer the right question for the right person (Fit)
**Outcome:** Marketing can answer "what did our paid spend deliver yesterday, broken by source?" in <30 seconds without asking Tomer. Management can answer "are we on track for the cohort target this quarter?" in <30 seconds.
**Success criteria:**
- 100% of marketing's top-5 daily questions are self-serve in the hub (per interviews).
- 100% of management's top-3 weekly questions are self-serve.
- Each persona's default landing tab answers their #1 question above the fold.
- Hub session telemetry shows marketing reaches their answer in ≤2 clicks for ≥80% of sessions.
**Dependencies:** Phase 1 trust floor (no point optimizing fit on numbers people don't trust) + UX Researcher's persona interviews (the 6 intake questions from v1-DRAFT, sharpened).
**Effort:** L (likely requires per-persona defaults, possibly per-persona views, plus surfacing currently-buried metrics).
**Risk if skipped:** Users open the hub once, can't find their answer, never come back. Adoption dies silently. Tomer remains the bottleneck and the rollout was theater.

### Phase 3 — Make it survive being shared (Governance + Self-serve)
**Outcome:** Marketing can onboard a new agency contributor in <30 min using documentation alone. Management can present a hub screen in a board meeting and answer the inevitable "where does that number come from?" without calling Tomer.
**Success criteria:**
- A published metric glossary covers 100% of Level-1 KPIs (definition, source query, refresh cadence, owner).
- A documented change-control process for adding/modifying any displayed number, with named owner.
- A documented incident process for "wrong number shipped" with named on-call.
- Read-only role with PII boundaries enforced for at least one external user (agency contractor) without security regression.
- "Where did this come from?" link on every Level-1 KPI card.
**Dependencies:** Phase 1 + Phase 2 must be stable. Cannot govern what's still being rebuilt. Also requires Tomer to ratify the ownership model — this is the only phase that needs a non-engineering decision from a human in power.
**Effort:** S (mostly docs + access controls + one PR per affected card).
**Risk if skipped:** Hub becomes Tomer-shaped tribal knowledge again the moment one new person joins. Every team change resets the trust clock. The whole project's durability depends on this phase shipping.

**Sequencing rationale:** Trust → Fit → Governance is the only order that compounds. Fit before Trust is lipstick on a wrong number. Governance before Fit governs the wrong thing. Each phase makes the next one cheaper and the prior one durable.

## 3. Outcome Measurement Framework

Distinguishing what we can **dogfood from the hub itself** (cheap, automatic, real-time) vs. what requires **observation** (qualitative, monthly cadence).

### Leading indicators (predict trouble early)

| # | Indicator | Source | Threshold for concern |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Hub sessions per marketer per week** (dogfoodable — instrument the hub) | Hub telemetry | <3/week by week 4 = adoption failing |
| L2 | **Slack/DM volume to Tomer asking "what does this number mean?"** (observed) | Manual tally, weekly | >5/week post-Phase-3 = governance docs aren't working |
| L3 | **Time-to-answer for top daily questions** (dogfoodable — instrument tab + click sequence) | Hub telemetry | >60s median by week 4 = fit failing |
| L4 | **Number of "shadow analytics" artifacts spotted in Slack/Notion/sheets** (observed) | Weekly scan | >2 new ones/month = hub doesn't serve a real need |
| L5 | **Trust incidents per month** (dogfoodable — incident log) | Incident doc | >0 P0 after Phase 1 = trust floor not actually built |

### Lagging indicators (confirm value)

| # | Indicator | Source | Target at 90 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | **% of paid-channel budget decisions traceable to a hub view** (observed via standup notes) | Marketing standup retrospective | ≥80% |
| G2 | **Tomer's hours/week answering data questions** (observed) | Tomer self-report | ↓ ≥50% vs. pre-rollout baseline |
| G3 | **Stakeholder NPS for the hub** (surveyed at 30/60/90d) | Direct survey, n=full team | ≥+30 by 90d |

**The most important measurement decision:** instrument the hub itself in Phase 1, before anyone uses it. We cannot evaluate rollout health from vibes, and we will not have a second chance to set a baseline. The hub measuring itself is the cheapest, most honest signal we will ever get — and it's a fraction of a day's work to add.

## 4. Stakeholder Alignment Risks (Top 5, Ranked)

These are the failure modes that lose the rollout for non-technical reasons. Each has a recommended mitigation, but the synthesizer should decide which mitigations land in the v2 plan.

### Risk 1 — The "CEO-vs-spreadsheet" trust war
**Pattern:** Management opens the hub. The total-users number says 16,790. The spreadsheet they've trusted for six months says 17,400. They don't know which is right but they trust the spreadsheet because they've used it longer. The hub loses by default.
**Why this is the #1 risk:** Trust is asymmetric. The hub has to be right AND prove it. The spreadsheet just has to be familiar.
**Mitigation lane:** Phase 1 must ship with reconciliation evidence visible on-screen (not buried in docs) for the highest-stakes numbers. "How we count users" link next to the number, comparing methodology vs. the legacy artifact.

### Risk 2 — "Another tool I didn't ask for" from marketing
**Pattern:** Marketing already uses GA4, X analytics, KOL spreadsheets, Snag. The hub looks like a 5th surface. They open it twice, don't find their specific question, never come back.
**Mitigation lane:** Phase 2 must interview marketing first about the questions they CURRENTLY can't answer in any tool — and lead with those. Not the questions the hub already happens to answer.

### Risk 3 — Shadow analytics from the inevitable feature gap
**Pattern:** Marketing wants cost-per-holder by campaign. Hub doesn't have ad-spend data yet. Marketer builds a Notion page. Other marketers start using it. Within 6 weeks the Notion page is the source of truth for paid-media decisions and the hub is bypassed.
**Mitigation lane:** Phase 3 governance must include a documented "request a metric" intake path with a public SLA, even if the SLA is "we'll respond in 5 business days." Visibility of the queue is what prevents shadow tooling, not speed of delivery.

### Risk 4 — Agency contractor access vs. PII exposure
**Pattern:** Paid-media rollout brings in an agency. They need hub access to optimize campaigns. The hub today shows wallet addresses, user counts, and identity-stitch data. Giving an agency a passcode that sees PII is a compliance problem; not giving them access means marketing becomes the human API. Both options break the rollout.
**Mitigation lane:** Phase 3 must include a read-only role with PII masking as a hard requirement, not a follow-up. The agency must be unblockable without exposing user-level identifiers. This is the only Phase 3 item that may need to ship earlier if agency onboarding is on the critical path for paid-spend ramp.

### Risk 5 — Management wants a PDF, not a hub
**Pattern:** The CEO doesn't want to open a URL. They want a Friday email or a slide. The hub is technically correct but operationally wrong for the highest-stakes audience. Management starts ignoring it within a month.
**Mitigation lane:** This is a packaging risk, not a product risk. Phase 2 should include at least one async digest (weekly email or auto-generated slide) sourced from the hub — same numbers, different surface. The hub is the source; the digest is the interface for users who won't open a tab.

**The risk NOT to forget:** all five of these are political/operational. Zero of them are solved by better engineering. The PM's job in v2 is to make sure each risk has a named owner in the rollout plan — not just a mitigation.

## Notes for the Synthesizer

- Phase 1 trust work and Phase 2 discovery interviews can run in parallel; they're independent. Tomer's calendar is the only shared resource.
- Risk 4 (agency PII) may force a Phase 3 governance item to ship inside Phase 2's timeline. Flag this as a sequencing escalation if agency onboarding has a hard date.
- The leading indicators in section 3 require hub-self-instrumentation that doesn't exist today. That's a Phase 1 engineering line item the Workflow Architect should pick up.
- Strongly recommend the v2 plan publishes its own "what we're NOT doing" list — mobile, real-time alerts to Slack, multi-tenant views, custom dashboard builder, etc. — to prevent scope creep from the inevitable "while you're in there..." requests.
